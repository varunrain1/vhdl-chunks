What you are looking at

Poles (red ×) — locations where the denominator of H(z) is zero. They create peaks / resonances in the frequency response. Closer to the unit circle → sharper peak. Must stay inside the unit circle for stability.

Zeros (green ○) — locations where the numerator is zero. They create notches / attenuation. A zero exactly on the unit circle forces a perfect null at that frequency.

Unit circle — the contour z = ejω. Evaluating H(z) here gives the ordinary frequency response that you hear / measure.

Coefficients — the bk (numerator) and am (denominator) of the difference equation. They are obtained by expanding the pole-zero form into powers of z−1. The recursive filter is then: y[n] = b0x[n] + b1x[n−1] + … − a1y[n−1] − a2y[n−2] − …

Example signal — the impulse response of this filter is a complicated combination of two damped complex exponentials (the natural modes set by the poles), shaped by the zeros. Moving poles changes the decay rates and oscillation frequencies of that impulse response; moving zeros changes how the filter colours any input.
